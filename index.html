About

The Long Way Round

My path into genomics started with chemistry, during my undergrad and master's. Those years were less about pipettes and more about developing a way of thinking: precise, quantitative, obsessed with the behavior of matter at small scales. But somewhere along the way, the molecules I most wanted to understand started moving on their own.

Chemistry taught me to ask "what is it and how much?", but biology kept interrupting with "why does it do that?" That tension pulled me toward the molecular logic of living systems, and eventually toward genomics, where you can ask both questions at once, across thousands of genes simultaneously.

My PhD at UBC centered on a fascinating protein borrowed from tardigrades (the near-indestructible "water bears") and what happens when it's expressed in yeast. The work spanned molecular biology, classic genetics, genome-wide Bar-seq screening, transcriptomics and deep bioinformatic analysis, and ended up featured in New Scientist.

Along the way I contributed to NASA's Artemis I mission, studying how eukaryotic cells respond to deep-space radiation, work under revision at Cell. I'm now interested in the places where large-scale data and genomics overlap, particularly in drug synergy, systems biology, and understanding complex diseases like cancer.

Research

Areas of Focus

Functional Genomics & High-Throughput Screening

Genome-wide chemical genetics, Bar-seq screens, and genetic interaction mapping. Designing and analyzing large-scale assays to understand how genes and drugs interact at scale.

Antifungal Drug Synergy

Systematic prediction and characterization of drug combinations in Candida and Saccharomyces species. Leveraging chemogenomics to identify synergistic interactions with therapeutic potential.

Stress Biology & DNA Damage Response

Exploring how exotic protective proteins — like the tardigrade Dsup — reshape the DNA damage landscape in eukaryotic cells. Also contributing to space biology research on cellular responses to cosmic radiation aboard NASA's Artemis I.

Bioinformatics & Multi-Omic Integration

Building reproducible pipelines (nf-core) for NGS data; differential expression, pathway analysis, and genetic interaction network interpretation — bridging wet lab experiments with computational insight.
